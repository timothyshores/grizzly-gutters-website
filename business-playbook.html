What a gutter contractor actually earns

You have to know his numbers to price anything. Here's what a gutter job is worth to him.
JobWhat the homeowner pays
Seamless gutter install$8–17 per foot. Typical house is 150–200 feet, so $1,200–3,400 a job
Gutter guards$1,500–4,500 whole house
Gutter cleaning$150–250 a visit
Copper (high end)$30–50 per foot
His margin is 20–40%, which is high for the trades — roofing and general construction run 10–15%. So on a typical $2,000 install he keeps roughly $400–800.

Read that number again, because it's the whole pitch: one job nets him more than our entire $500 website costs. One job a year covers a $150/month retainer four times over.

Cost per job he actually books, after the ones that don't close: $150–667. On Angi, roughly $667. He is paying that today, every month, forever.

Our $500 website is a one-time cost less than one booked Angi job, and it sends him leads nobody else is racing him for. Our $100–150/month is two or three Angi leads — not per job, per lead, most of which never close.
How to use this on a call: ask what he's paying Angi or Thumbtack right now. He'll tell you, and he'll be annoyed about it — contractors hate shared leads. Then the pitch writes itself: "this costs less than one of those leads, and you're not competing with five other guys for it."

Don't lead with this. Ask first, let him say the number out loud, then land it.

Podium vs GoHighLevel — what's the difference?

They get mentioned together but they are not the same kind of thing at all.

GoHighLevel is a tool for agencies to resell. Podium is a product sold straight to the business owner. That's the whole difference.

If we bought GoHighLevel, we'd pay $297/month and put Jason, Josh, and eight more contractors inside it as sub-accounts, each one thinking it's our software. If Jason bought Podium, he'd pay $450+/month directly and we'd have nothing to do with it.

Why we do neither: GoHighLevel means paying $297/month before anyone has paid us, and our margin becomes whatever we can charge above their fee. Tim builds the same thing for a few dollars a month, so we keep the whole margin instead of renting it.

Booking appointments — the honest answer

This one surprised us. The research argues against building it.
Homeowners who call83% still prefer phoning a contractor over booking online
Prefer online bookingOnly 12%
Phone vs formPhone leads convert 10–15x better than web forms
Speed matters more78% hire whoever calls back within 5 minutes
A gutter estimate can't be booked like a haircut. You have to measure the house before quoting. So a calendar slot isn't really booking a job — it's booking a visit that still needs a phone conversation to qualify. Skip that conversation and you send a truck to a house you can't actually quote.

So the biggest win isn't a booking calendar. It's calling back fast. That's what the auto-text and instant lead alert already do, and it's why they come first on the ladder.
That said, booking is still worth offering — as a fallback, not the main event. Roughly 15–20% of people won't call: they're browsing at 10pm, or they're younger and just don't phone strangers. A "pick a time" button captures those instead of losing them.

Cal.com's free tier does this at $0 — unlimited bookings, email and text reminders, drops into the site we already built. Nothing to host, nothing to maintain.

What we will NOT do is compete with Jobber. Jobber is real scheduling software for contractors — $29/month to start, $99/month once you want automatic text reminders and two-way texting. If a client wants a full job calendar with dispatching, that's Jobber's job, and the right answer is to say so. We're not going to underprice a real product with a worse version of it.

Don't discount. If he balks, split it: "$250 now, $250 when you're happy." Never drop the number. Dropping it says you made the first one up.

The one thing that will kill a monthly client

43% of all churn happens in the first 90 days — before the work has had time to prove itself. And the #1 reason clients leave isn't price, it's feeling like nothing is happening.

So the first month matters more than the next twelve. Get him a real, visible lead in week one and say something about it. A client who sees one lead come through stays for years — retainer clients average 56 months. A client who hears nothing for 30 days cancels.

Your job after the sale isn't nothing. Text him when a lead comes in. "Did that one turn into a job?" That single question is most of retention.

Why volume, not big fish

Ten clients at $500 beats one at $5,000, because the ten are easy yeses and the one takes three months of meetings and a proposal deck.

And gutters aren't special. Roofing, landscaping, HVAC, concrete, fencing — same pitch, same site, different photos. Once the gutter version works, it's a template we point at any local trade.

The websites are the door, not the business. One-time work means starting from zero every month. The recurring $150 is what actually compounds — month 13 builds on month 12 instead of replacing it. Sell the site to get in the door; the monthly is the real product.
